# Disk Health Check

## Overview

Before installing Windows, the storage device must be inspected to ensure it is reliable and healthy. Installing an operating system on a failing drive can lead to installation errors, poor performance, data corruption, and future system instability.

---

## Objectives

- Verify the health of the storage device.
- Detect possible hardware failures.
- Prevent Windows installation on damaged drives.
- Recommend replacement when necessary.

---

## Identify the Storage Type

Determine the type of storage installed in the device.

Examples:

- SATA HDD
- SATA SSD
- NVMe SSD
- M.2 SATA SSD

---

## Health Inspection

Check the following:

- SMART status
- Overall health percentage
- Drive temperature
- Read/Write errors
- Reallocated sectors
- Pending sectors
- Bad sectors

---

## Recommended Tools

- CrystalDiskInfo
- Hard Disk Sentinel
- Manufacturer diagnostic tools

---

## Warning Signs

Do not continue with Windows installation if the drive shows:

- Critical SMART warnings
- Large number of bad sectors
- Unusual clicking noises (HDD)
- Extremely low health
- Frequent read/write failures

Recommend replacing the drive before continuing.

---

## SSD Considerations

For SSDs, verify:

- Remaining health
- Available free space
- Firmware version
- Temperature

---

## HDD Considerations

For HDDs, verify:

- Mechanical noise
- Bad sectors
- Read speed
- SMART status

---

## Best Practices

- Always check disk health before formatting.
- Never ignore SMART warnings.
- Explain the results to the customer.
- Document any detected issues.

---

## Checklist

- Storage type identified
- SMART status checked
- Health verified
- Temperature checked
- Bad sectors inspected
- Customer informed
